## Act I — Domesticity, Not Disruption

### Chapter 1: Why One File Rules the Haus

Sanctum began in a living room, not a venture-backed pitch deck. As [What Is Sanctum?](/getting-started/what-is-sanctum/) and [A Day in the Haus](/getting-started/a-day-in-the-haus/) lay out, a modern haushold runs two completely different lives on the same closet silicon: family curfews, lights, and calendars on one side; private work, research, and code on the other.

On 19 March 2026 (`9c8640e`), the first formal doctrine landed — and three days later the `bridge100` cascade immediately stress-tested it with maximum chaos. As recorded in [The Living Force](/architecture/living-force/) and [Operational History](/operations/operational-history/), services restarting blindly against missing dependencies just poured gasoline on outages. Every port, path, agent seat, and schedule moved into one `instance.yaml`, separating declarative topology from 3 AM folklore.

### Chapter 2: Promises That Hold at Curfew

Nowhere is honesty harder than family screen time — teenagers are basically red-team operators with infinite free time. Under [Haus-Control](/architecture/screen-time/), a curfew command that prints exit zero while Fortnite keeps running is worse than having no rule at all.

That discipline drove the November 2026 console playtime series across [Nintendo Switch](/parents-guide/mirrors/nintendo-switch/), [Xbox & PlayStation](/parents-guide/mirrors/xbox-playstation/), and [More Time](/parents-guide/more-time/). Every mirror reads back vendor or router state directly instead of taking an optimistic button press on faith, while [Shared Hardware Identity](/operations/device-identity-shared-hardware/) binds dual-NIC consoles to one target whether they sneak onto Wi-Fi or Ethernet.

## Act II — The Machine That Learned Not to Lie

### Chapter 3: The Pressure Valve and Silent Failure

April and May taught the haus how self-healing turns into self-sabotage when probes watch the wrong gauge. The first dated field note, [2026-04-07: Provider Overhaul](/operations/2026-04-07-provider-overhaul/), unified five provider paths and Signal delivery, and two weeks later [The Pressure Valve Trilogy](/operations/2026-04-20-the-pressure-valve-trilogy/) logged one hall-of-fame disaster: a midnight kernel panic, a Rust memory daemon shipped by lunch, and that exact daemon bouncing the service it was hired to protect by dinner. (Maximum effort. Wrong target.)

Worse still was [Seventeen Days of Silent Failure](/operations/2026-05-16-seventeen-days-of-silent-failure/), where logs proudly printed `apply_started` for seventeen straight days without ever recording `apply_completed`, followed by [The Eight-Item Sweep](/operations/2026-06-05-the-eight-item-sweep/) that cleared the lingering config and iCloud-sync traps. Principle 8 hardened into granite: verify the physical effect after the action, never the polite exit code of the trigger.

### Chapter 4: R2D2, Force Flow, and the Ladder

Out of those scars grew a real immune system. [Force Flow](/architecture/force-flow/) became the `:4077` notification hub so agents never wake an operator with raw JSON vomit, while [R2D2](/architecture/r2d2/) formalized an 11-layer safety stack with cooldowns, circuit breakers, and explicit verification rungs.

Instead of blindly kickstarting dying daemons into infinity, [The Ladder That Heals Itself](/operations/2026-05-10-the-ladder-that-heals-itself/) escalates one bounded step at a time and stops cold when state refuses to move. A supervisor smart enough to stop looping is worth ten that look heroic at 03:00.

## Act III — The Cathedral Without a Meter

### Chapter 5: Subscription-First Routing

Most AI stacks default to metered API taxis and mail every family question straight to an external cloud. [Subscription First](/operations/2026-06-13-subscription-first/) flipped the script: local Metal inference handles everything private first, backed by flat-rate subscription bridges on `:3456` (Claude Max) and `:6543` (Gemini Ultra).

Standing guard out front, [The Smart Router Cathedral](/architecture/smart-router-cathedral/) on `:8055` enforces a five-tier defense ladder across four backends without ever handing raw credentials to downstream agents. Local [Cilghal](/agents/cilghal/) and [Windu](/agents/windu/) lanes keep haushold telemetry locked inside the walls.

### Chapter 6: Don't Grade Your Own Homework

State and evaluation needed the exact same paranoia. [Memory Vault](/guides/memory-vault/) built a git-native `:42069` knowledge store across sessions, reinforced in [The Post-Quantum Vault Ceremony](/operations/2026-06-20-the-post-quantum-vault/) with X-Wing ML-KEM-768 encryption and documented in [Sanctum Vault Armor](/architecture/vault-armor/) for before-first-unlock and after-first-unlock APFS boundaries.

In July, [The Ruler Was Broken, Not the Model](/operations/2026-07-26-the-ruler-was-broken/) exposed why earlier local benchmarks lied straight to our faces: keyword-echo rubrics rewarded bloated essays over right answers. Swapping self-grading for blind pairwise judges fixed the ruler instead of blaming the weights.

## Act IV — Zero Python, Pure Rust, Post-Quantum Locks

### Chapter 7: Service Principals and Millisecond Redaction

By late summer, hardening moved from shell scripts to OS walls and compiled binaries. [Service Principal Wave-1](/operations/2026-08-07-service-principal-wave-1/) quarantined daemons under a dedicated `sanctum` service user instead of letting background jobs roam loose under an interactive operator login.

Next, [The Weight of Names: Pure Rust Presidio](/operations/2026-08-25-the-weight-of-names-pure-rust-presidio/) evicted a 1,450 MB Python NLP monster for a 3.8 MB native Rust binary scrubbing PII in under 1 ms. By October, [The Fleet Learned Post-Quantum SSH](/operations/2026-10-28-the-fleet-learned-post-quantum-ssh/) rolled OpenSSH 10.6p1 with `mlkem768x25519-sha256` across ports `:2222` and `:2223` on every node. (Yes, quantum-resistant SSH for a closet Mac Mini. Windu slept great that night.)

### Chapter 8: Native Metal Inference

The biggest runtime jump landed in September with [Zero-Python AI Across the Estate](/operations/2026-09-22-zero-python-ai-estate/) and [Inco Hacks Native: DFlash 2 & SSM Prefix Ladders](/operations/2026-09-22-inco-dflash2-speculative-inference/). Inside `sanctum-rs`, [`sanctum-mlx`](/architecture/sanctum-mlx/) moved model serving, speculative DFlash 2 drafting, SSM prefix caching, and local speech (`sanctum-stt` and `sanctum-tts`) onto native Rust and Metal.

Yanking Python out of the hot serving path erased entire species of virtualenv drift and cold-boot surprises. One static binary now feeds both the council and health lanes under hard memory ceilings.

## Act V — From Fortress to Product

### Chapter 9: One Binary, One Installer, Thirty-Nine Commands

A fortress only its original wizard can rebuild is a hobby, not a platform. Starting with [The Installer the Friend Can Trust](/operations/2026-05-22-the-installer-the-friend-can-trust/) and [The Wheel That Wouldn't Move](/operations/2026-06-19-the-wheel-that-wouldnt-move/), and culminating in [The Fortress That Became a Product](/operations/2026-10-31-the-fortress-that-became-a-product/), every hardcoded local shortcut gave way to generated manifests and verified symlinks.

Today [`sanctum-cli`](/architecture/sanctum-cli/) ships as Sanctum `v1.0.0` (public beta) with 39 commands, over 2,800 passing tests, and keyless Sigstore OIDC release provenance — installed cleanly via `sanctum.run/install.sh` or [homebrew-sanctum](https://github.com/Ogilthorp3/homebrew-sanctum). Across the public 1.0 release surface, [sanctum-cli](https://github.com/Ogilthorp3/sanctum-cli) and [homebrew-sanctum](https://github.com/Ogilthorp3/homebrew-sanctum) ship under `FSL-1.1-Apache-2.0`, while [sanctum-docs](https://github.com/Ogilthorp3/sanctum-docs) publishes prose and art under `CC-BY-4.0` and site code under `MIT` alongside the consumer-facing [sanctum-haus](https://github.com/Ogilthorp3/sanctum-haus) site. Private runtime and operational repositories (`sanctum-rs`, `sanctum-config`, `sanctum-vault`, and `Claude_Code`) stay cleanly separated from the public 1.0 surface.

## Act VI — The Dragon and the Five-Day Watch

### Chapter 10: Narrow Adapter, Three-Brain Council

Year One closes on the Dragon campaign: training a laser-focused LoRA adapter (`R1`) specifically for [Windu](/agents/windu/) and [Cilghal](/agents/cilghal/) without dulling general fluency. [The Dragon and the Heads That Never Forgot](/operations/2026-11-08-the-dragon-and-the-heads-that-never-forgot/) caught a float32 decay bug and vaulted exam accuracy from `0.4821` to `0.6741`, followed by [One Shaft, Two Escapements](/operations/2026-11-10-one-shaft-two-escapements/) (permuted 4-bit weights sharing ANE and GPU memory), [Two Beats on the Clocktower Spindle](/operations/2026-11-13-two-beats-on-the-clocktower-spindle/) (32-token GatedDeltaNet checkpointing for the coder seat), and [The Compressor Under the Wrong Indent](/operations/2026-11-15-the-compressor-under-the-wrong-indent/) (`lean-ctx` convergence across the fleet).

2. **Three-Brain Council & Cutover (`WA2`, 17 Nov):** [The Dragon Takes the Seat](/operations/2026-11-17-the-dragon-takes-the-seat/) captures `21:38:10` restart, nine runbook faults caught before and during the window, and the 45-minute long-context gate (`7 × 1.0`).
