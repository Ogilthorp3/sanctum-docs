The headline number is the gate. The phantom that the promotion pipeline had been comparing against for the last fourteen-plus nights was `0.881`, derived from the original 26-case screen tier that produced the May-12 champion. The honest 109-case full-tier rescore on the same champion adapter returned `overall = 0.7033`, and the calibrator wrote `calibrated_gate = 0.7000` into `~/.sanctum/state/production-champion.json`. The `promote_champion.sh --print-effective-gate` invocation now returns `effective_gate=0.7 source=calibrated_gate` instead of `effective_gate=0.881 source=env_GATE_SCORE`. Eighteen points of imaginary ceiling came off.

## Why the phantom was high

The 26-case screen tier was easier than the 109-case full tier. The screen was the gold core — short questions with single-master answers, no cross-agent routing complexity, no real-world reasoning under partial information. The May-12 champion scored `0.881` on that subset because the subset rewarded exactly what the May-12 champion was good at, and the full tier evaluates the champion against the dimensions the screen never tested. The drop from `0.881` to `0.7033` is not a regression; it's the same model passing through a harder lens.

The autoresearch pipeline, however, was comparing nightly candidates' full-tier scores against the screen-tier ceiling. Every candidate had to clear `0.881` on the harder tier to promote. The phantom rejected a population of legitimately-better adapters silently for two weeks. The [June 2 post](./2026-06-02-the-gate-that-was-wrong-for-two-weeks/) built the calibrator that closed this gap; the June 5 wait was the GPU lock; the calibrator finally fired in the small hours of the sixth.

## The per-master cells

The same calibration run wrote the [Stone 1 floor's](./2026-06-04-the-aggregate-hid-two-weak-masters/) missing baseline. Eight masters, each scored individually:

The aggregate `0.7033` is the arithmetic mean of the eight cells. The variance across the cells is 17.8 points between Windu's `0.789` and Ahsoka's `0.611`. The per-master floor is now `max(0.50, master_baseline − 0.10)`. So a future candidate must clear `0.531` on Ahsoka, `0.556` on Mundi, `0.583` on Quigon, `0.567` on Mothma — and `0.689` on Windu, `0.658` on Jocasta. The strong-master cells require strong-candidate performance to match. A candidate can no longer ride a high Windu score to mask a collapsed Ahsoka score.

The Stone 1 sidecar is the rule that prevents an aggregate from carrying a weak cell across the gate. Tonight's nightly autoresearch will be the first run where the rule actually fires on promotion checks.

## What changes for the autoresearch loop tonight

Two things shift, and they shift in opposite directions.

First, more candidates pass the absolute number. A nightly that produced a candidate at `0.715` would have been rejected against the phantom; against the honest `0.7000` it is promotion-eligible on the aggregate. The pipeline has been silently throwing away winners for the entire run length of the phantom.

Second, more candidates fail the per-master cells. A candidate at `0.715` aggregate that dropped Ahsoka to `0.500` — because dropout-tuning hurt the family-secondary surface, say — would have promoted against an aggregate-only gate but now stalls at `per_master_floor_veto: ahsoka floor=0.531 got=0.500`. The breadcrumb names the cell that killed the promotion.

Net effect: the gate is more permissive on the easy direction (the absolute number drops 18 points) and stricter on the hard direction (every cell must clear its own floor). The new gate's surface is shaped like the actual eight-dimension behavior the council needs, not the one-number summary that hid Ahsoka and Mundi.

## The watcher pattern, briefly

The retry-watcher v2 (`/tmp/calibration-retry-watcher-v2.sh`, armed on the MBP at 19:12 with a 4-hour subprocess timeout) was the simplest of the things that made this work. Five-minute poll, two probes (`carmack_eval` and `mlx_lm.lora --train`), break-and-fire on a clean read. The 5-minute interval added at most five minutes of delay to a 63-minute calibration; the entire chain — wait, fire, score, sidecar, state update — completed in under three hours of wall clock from the moment the watcher armed. No supervisor, no daemon, no plist; just a bash script and a five-minute sleep. The autoresearch nightly never noticed; calibration never noticed; the per-master backfill ran immediately on success. The boring tool worked at exactly the boundary it was designed for.
