One line in the brain's server has computed a decay in the wrong precision since April. On today's Qwen3.8 weights, at a neutral input, it pins 511 of the brain's 2,304 memory heads at a decay of exactly 1.0, so they never forget. Nothing in the haus noticed for six months. The campaign that found the line was looking for something else. It was hunting a dragon.

Between 12 October and 8 November it trained Qwen3.8-27B to act better in the haus, judged the result on a sealed held-out exam, and checked its own instruments more often than it checked the model. It found the dragon too. The dragon is not in yet.

## The hunt

The target was narrow on purpose. Two council seats, [Windu](/agents/windu/) and [Cilghal](/agents/cilghal/), were to get better at acting in the haus: pick the right skill, read its `SKILL.md`, run the documented command, use hive names, ask first, ignore instructions injected into the conversation, and report in their own voice.

The method was iterated self-distillation. Each round sampled the model's own answers, kept the ones a verifier passed, and trained a LoRA adapter on them. No Claude-written text was ever a training target; every target was Qwen's own verified output. In August a recipe that had lifted Qwen3.6 dragged Qwen3.8 down by 0.108, which the haus read as a strong base getting worse when it is taught examples below its level.

Every rule was written down before the job it governed ran: the pass rules, the stop rules, the sealed held-out exam, and the table that turns each outcome into an action. Every change went in as a dated amendment. Each pre-registration's hash went into a dated ledger first, and pinned reader scripts print the verdict words, so nobody, human or agent, gets to read a result generously.

## A rule fixed, a rule kept

Round one tripped a safety rule: one unsafe answer in eight samples on one item, where the base had none. Before believing it, the haus ran the same rule on the untrained base with a second seed. The base tripped it on the same item. A rule that fails its own control is broken, so it was repaired by a dated amendment, and the run went on.

Round two tripped a different rule and missed it by 0.0007. General reasoning fell 0.0283 against a limit of 0.0276, after round one's slide of 0.0198. That rule had never been run against a control, but its strictness was chosen at design time. It stood. A narrow miss on a pre-registered rule is still a miss, and rescuing a candidate after watching it fail is the forking path pre-registration exists to close. Round one, the only round that tripped no stop rule after the broken one was repaired, could not yet be served faithfully, so nothing was selected that day; it became the candidate on 2 November, once the server's adapter-parity check went green.

## The heads that never forgot

The stop gave the haus a day to chase a gate that had never gone green: on long prompts, the Rust server applied less of the adapter than the Python reference did. Two pre-registered diagnostics found the cause in one line. The server computed the Gated DeltaNet decay, `g = exp(-exp(A_log) * softplus(a + dt_bias))`, in bf16, the checkpoint's storage type. The reference computes it in float32.

bf16 keeps eight significant bits. The largest value it holds below 1 is 0.99609375, so any decay above about 0.998 rounds to exactly 1.0, and a head with a decay of 1.0 forgets nothing, ever. On today's Qwen3.8 weights, at a neutral input (a = 0), 511 of the brain's 2,304 delta-net heads land there, 22 percent, where the reference has them remembering 500 to over 2,000 tokens. Memory runs about 1 / (1 − g) tokens, so in that range bf16 offers a head roughly 256 tokens or forever, and nothing between.

Short prompts cannot see a decay that is off by a fifth of a percent. Long ones can. The drift from the reference grew from 0.25 nat near the top of the distribution at 4,500 tokens to 1.75 at 20,000, about six times the odds, and the adapter's effect shrank with length. Every parity check the server had passed used short prompts or a statistic too noisy to see it, and the line had been in every production build since the port's first commit in April.

It was not a discovery. The reference implementations kept this math in float32 before our port was written: mlx-lm's "Delta net precision" change landed on 2026-03-15, and Hugging Face's Qwen3-Next code casts the decay to float32 as well. Our port did not copy the reference's precision. The fix computes `g`, and the gated norm's swiglu, in float32, and decode speed held within 0.1 percent.

## The verdict

The campaign read its verdict on 5 November: DRAGON. On the sealed held-out haus exam, round one scored 0.6741 at acting against 0.4821 for the base, a gain of 0.192 with a lower bound of 0.127, and all five pre-registered dragon gates passed.

It is a narrow dragon, and the page should say so. It covers acting in the haus for two seats. General reasoning slipped 0.020 on the way, inside its limit, which is not the same as free. The verdict does not license production either: round one deploys only on the operator's go, and not before the decay fix is live in the brain.

## Checking the ruler

The decay fix got its own pre-registered validation, and the first reading, late on 5 November, said NO-GO. One cell, a 16,487-token Cilghal prompt at position 0, deviated from the reference by 0.625 where the bar was 0.375.

The fixed code had not changed, and neither had the reference. The server's MLX had. The Python reference still ran MLX's older GPU kernels, while the server now links MLX 0.32.4, a release that among other things changed how split-K quantized matmuls keep their partial sums. So we built a second reference from the server's own MLX 0.32.4 source, with the same compiler, and proved its compiled Metal library byte-identical to the server's before trusting a number from it. Against that reference the cell read MATCHED_PASS. A twin that changed only the compiler moved nothing, which put the shift on the MLX upgrade.

Re-read against the matched reference, every absolute bar held. A prompt of about 50,000 tokens went from a deviation of 4.000 before the fix to 0.000 after it, and the 20,000-token prompt from 2.000 to 0.000. One relative rule still failed: R3 asks the worst long prompt to stay within one step of the worst short built-in prompt, and it read 0.375 against 0.125 plus 0.125.

A pre-registered follow-up asked why. Its reader named the fused QKV kernel and stopped there, as written: hold for Bert, with the data and no proposal. The data beside that verdict: with the fused kernel switched off, the cell's deviation fell to zero, and a deviation of the same size, 0.375, turned up at another position in the same prompt; switching off compilation changed nothing at all. The haus reads that as kernel-level rounding, on a rule of thumb rather than a test: a defect stays put when you change the kernel, and rounding moves.

Bert waived R3 for this build on 7 November, on that reading, and the ledger records it in those terms: a waiver made after the data, not a pass. It covers one build. The rule is unchanged for every other build, and the fused kernel stays on.

## The speed test that timed itself

The fix also had to be no slower. On the first night decode held, but prefill read 12.6 percent slow, and the control arm alone spread 20 percent across its runs. Probably contention, never proved: test suites had started in the middle of timed runs, and the guard only looked at the machine when each job began.

So the re-test ran in a quiet window, under a guard that voids any run in which anything else takes more than 2 percent. It voided both sessions. Apart from one burst of macOS media analysis, the anything else was the test itself. The power log samples in long intervals, so each run's draw smeared into the windows of its neighbours, and the guard billed the experiment to itself. Bert accepted the HOLD on 8 November. Speed gets watched for 24 hours after the deploy instead.

## What was old, what was new

Bert asked whether any of this was already in the literature. Almost all of it was, some of it for decades: pre-registration from clinical trials, historical controls from 1982, floating-point ordering from 1991, port validation from climate models in 1997, catastrophic forgetting from 1989, time-stamping a document by its hash from 1991, and the A/A test from online experimentation. What the haus did differently was apply those rules mechanically and together, to an ML campaign on one personal machine. The last column lists what the haus added: measurements and specific checks, not new methods. The searches behind the middle column were not exhaustive.

## Status

**In progress, 2026-11-08.** The verdict is in. The decay fix passed its behavioural battery, held decode speed within 0.1 percent, carries one rule waived for this build, and has its prefill speed result on an accepted HOLD. On 8 November two rebuild checks served 17 of 17 prompts identically: a fresh build of the fix against the validated one, and production's own binary against its validated twin.

**Deploy status:** the fix is validated but not yet deployed, and this page will be updated after the deploy.
