The Wi-Fi bars on the MacBook were full. The lease on `10.0.0.x` was fresh. Nothing on the LAN could reach the outside world. Every sixty-seven minutes, Qui-Gon's doctor woke up on `manoir.local`, diagnosed a dead WAN, and pulled the plug again.

We had switched the Bell GigaHub into pure PPPoE Bridge Mode so the Firewalla Gold Pro could dial `pppoe0` directly and own the public IP without Bell's `/1` netmask games. The bridge held. The healers we built for the old DHCP world did not know that.

## A hollow policy table and a silent drop

When `pppoe0` redialed after a night fiber blip, the Linux kernel dutifully installed `default dev pppoe0` in `table main`. The Firewalla does not route LAN traffic from `table main` alone. Its policy router consults rule `32765`, which looks up `table global_default` before falling through.

That table was empty. Worse, when `firemain` exited during the flap, it left the kernel's `FORWARD` chain policy set to `DROP` with zero jump rules, while `/home/pi/.router/config/dnsmasq/br0.resolv.conf` pointed at a vanished file. Every LAN device received `REFUSED` on DNS and a silent firewall drop on raw IPs.

Meanwhile, `firerouter`'s internal `PingTest` tried to verify WAN health by binding a socket to the public IP on `pppoe0` and pinging the peer gateway. Because `table global_default` lacked a default route, strict reverse-path filtering (`rp_filter`) rejected the probe. `firerouter` concluded the fiber was dead and tore `pppoe0` down in a loop.

## The doctor with the wrong textbook

On `manoir.local`, two watchdogs were watching the edge: `singlenat-watchdog.sh` and Qui-Gon's `fw-heal-forwarding.sh`. Both were written during [the slash-one war](/operations/2026-06-15-the-slash-one-that-ate-the-lan/), when `eth0` carried a DHCP lease from Bell's Advanced DMZ.

Their recovery playbook for a dark WAN was muscle memory: release the DHCP lease on `eth0`, request a fresh one, and restart `firerouter`. Under PPPoE Bridge Mode, `eth0` has no IPv4 address; it is the raw Ethernet carrier underneath `pppoe0`. Running `dhclient -r eth0` and restarting `firerouter` every hour did not heal the WAN. It killed the PPPoE session that was trying to recover. Twelve vault notes in a row recorded the same verdict: `WAN-LINK-DOWN`, `converged: false`.

<Aside type="danger" title="Never run dhclient on a PPPoE carrier">
When `ip route` or `/home/pi/.router/config/wan.json` says the WAN interface is `pppoe0` (`type: pppoe`), `eth0` is only the L2 transport. Releasing DHCP on `eth0` or bouncing `firerouter` tears down the PPPoE tunnel. Heal `pppoe0` in place instead.
</Aside>

## The incoming SYN-ACK cliff and the hotspot ambush

Two more traps waited once the tunnel came back. First, when we ran a multi-stream throughput test directly on the Firewalla Gold Pro (`10.0.0.1`), outbound TLS handshakes succeeded and the payload immediately stalled. Our CDN guard (`sanctum-mss-fastly-fix.sh`) clamped `TCPMSS` to `1400` on `FORWARD` and `OUTPUT`, which protected LAN clients and outbound `SYN` packets. It did not clamp `PREROUTING`. When Cloudflare replied to a process on the Firewalla itself, its incoming `SYN-ACK` announced `MSS 1460`, and the Firewalla's kernel emitted 1500-byte packets that died on the 1492-byte `pppoe0` wire.

Second, on the operator's MacBook Pro, iCloud Keychain had quietly synced the iPhone's personal hotspot SSID back into `en0`'s preferred network list while the phone was plugged in over USB-C (`en7`). Whenever home Wi-Fi flickered, macOS associated `en0` to the phone's Wi-Fi hotspot while `en7` was already tethered to the same phone over USB. Both interfaces claimed `172.20.10.0/28`, routing flapped between `en0` and `en7`, and long-lived SSE streams died with `write: broken pipe`.

## What landed

We replaced the one-shot DHCP assumptions with a four-layer PPPoE armor across the Firewalla, `manoir.local`, and the MacBook Pro:

1. **Five-second box sentinel (`sanctum-poison-sentinel.service`):** Runs `/home/pi/.firewalla/config/sanctum-route-poison-sentinel.sh` continuously on the Firewalla. Whenever `pppoe0` has an IPv4 address, it enforces `default dev pppoe0` in both `table global_default` and `table main`, flips `iptables -P FORWARD ACCEPT` if `firemain` leaves it `DROP`, and restores the `br0.resolv.conf` symlink if `dnsmasq` loses its upstream file.
2. **PPPoE reconnect & boot hooks (`/etc/ppp/ip-up.d/zz-sanctum-pppoe` and `post_main.sh`):** Immediately invoke `/home/pi/.firewalla/config/sanctum-pppoe-armor.sh` whenever `pppd` negotiates a session or the box reboots, disabling `rp_filter` on `pppoe0`/`eth0` and clamping `TCPMSS 1400` across `FORWARD`, `OUTPUT`, and `PREROUTING`.
3. **PPPoE-aware fleet healers:** Updated `singlenat-watchdog.sh` and `fw-heal-forwarding.sh` on `manoir.local` to detect `pppoe0` first and run `sanctum-pppoe-armor.sh` in place, never `dhclient -r`.
4. **Hotspot collision guard (`wifi-stability-sentinel.sh`):** On the MacBook Pro, the 3-minute sentinel strips the iPhone hotspot SSID from `en0` if iCloud Keychain re-syncs it, and keeps `Wi-Fi` (`#1`) ordered above `iPhone USB` (`#2`) whenever `en0` holds a `10.0.0.x` home lease.

With `PREROUTING` clamped and the sentinel active, we measured live PPPoE throughput directly on the Firewalla Gold Pro over its 10GbE `eth0` port:

```text
Firewalla Gold Pro (10GbE eth0 -> pppoe0 -> Cloudflare)   2.15 Gbps (2,147 Mbps)
MacBook Pro (Nepveu-6G Wi-Fi -> Orbi 960 -> Firewalla)    0.74 Gbps (  740 Mbps)
```

At **2.15 Gbps** over software PPPoE on the Gold Pro's Intel N97, pure Bridge Mode outruns every Wi-Fi radio and 1G/2.5G port in the haus—without touching Bell's Advanced DMZ or its `/1` poison route.
