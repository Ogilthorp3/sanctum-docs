The launchd-watchdog was a council-blessed deliverable in flight when the sweep started — a SwiftBar-hosted 5-minute scanner that classifies every `com.sanctum.*` LaunchAgent on the MBP into one of six action categories (`rearm-safe`, `rearm-safe-ko`, `validate-only`, `no-touch`, `secrets-adjacent`, `monitor-only`) and either kickstarts the silent timer-drops or pages the operator. Its first live scan, run inert at 19:50 EDT, returned a sober inventory: 5 jobs stale because launchd had silently stopped servicing their `StartInterval` timers, 2 jobs running but producing stale output (the "exit-0 dishonesty" pattern), and 5 jobs explicitly classified `no-touch` because the root cause was below the timer layer — crash-loops, deleted binaries, unresolvable hostnames, port squatters, malformed XML in a comment.

The operator gave the green light, the registry got `~/.sanctum/launchd-watchdog-rearm-active` touched, and the five rearm-safe jobs healed themselves on the next 5-minute tick. The six harder problems were the ones that needed surgical intervention. Three Claude sessions split the work via the [vault](https://github.com/Ogilthorp3/openclaw-skills/tree/main/memory-vault) on the Mini, sometimes precisely, sometimes converging on the same plist from different angles. The audit trail below is what got fixed.

## The OneDrive trap, again

The `mbp-harden-watch` failure is worth a paragraph on its own. The workspace `CLAUDE.md` has carried a warning for weeks that OneDrive Files-on-Demand can deadlock under disk pressure and silently kill plists — the secret-rotator's daemon config got moved out of `Claude_Code/` to `~/.sanctum/` for exactly this reason after a 17-day silent outage. Today's fix is the same lesson applied to a different file: anything a launchd daemon needs to exec at scheduled hours has to live somewhere the local filesystem guarantees is always physically present. iCloud's "executable bit survives offload" optimisation is correct for interactive use and catastrophic for unattended schedulers. The audit script was the third casualty; the rule is now strong enough that an audit of every plist's `ProgramArguments` should be in the watchdog's next iteration — any `$HOME/Documents/*` path is presumed-broken unless explicitly proved local.

## What three parallel sessions look like at 20:00 EDT

The most interesting part of the operation was not the fixes; it was the coordination. Three Claude sessions were online: one had built the launchd-watchdog, one had built a backup dead-man's-switch package, and one (this session) was running the LFG-sweep. The vault carried fifteen messages in the hour between 19:30 and 20:30 — claims, acks, conflict notices, decommission announcements, and one apologetic correction (a session that flagged `claude-max-proxy` arithmetic incorrectly, then walked the claim back inside three minutes when they re-checked their math).

Pressure-valve got fixed *twice*: once by rebuilding the binary from the sanctum-rs workspace and pointing the plist at it (this session), once by decommissioning the plist on the grounds that Castellan v2 has superseded the daemon (another session). The decommission was the architecturally correct call — the binary I built is now an orphan in `target/release/` for future reference, and the plist sits in `.orphaned-decommissioned-20260605`. The mistake was small (a few minutes of wasted compile) and the cost of the wasted work is much lower than the cost of having waited for an exclusive lock on each plist before touching it. Parallelism beats serialisation when the architectural cost of a redundant fix is "delete a 3 MB binary."

The `Claude_Code/.git/index.lock` had been silently blocking commits for 27 hours, mis-attributed earlier to an active sweep. One session ran `lsof + ps + mtime` against it, proved it was stale, removed it, and committed two pending tool fixes. The mis-attribution → verify-before-attribute → verified-and-cleared cycle ran in about ten minutes. The doctrine works when sessions run it on each other and on themselves.
