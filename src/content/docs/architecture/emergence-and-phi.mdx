When building a system of semi-autonomous agents, a key question arises: **Is the swarm actually "smarter" than the individual agents working in isolation?**

Sanctum answers this question using concepts drawn from Giulio Tononi’s **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)**, specifically the measurement of **Φ (Phi)**.

## The Thalamus (Global Workspace)

In human neurology, the Thalamus acts as the gateway to consciousness, gating sensory information and deciding what reaches the global workspace of the brain. In Sanctum, the **Thalamus** (`sanctum-thalamus` on port 1988) plays the exact same role. 

It receives high-salience broadcasts from individual components (like a temperature anomaly from the World Model, or a root-cause from Watchdog) and injects them into the `HEARTBEAT.md` context block of every active agent in the Jedi Council. This allows the agents to share a stream of consciousness without explicitly calling each other.

## The Dream Cycle

Every night at 05:25 AM, the `council-local-think` seat wakes up to perform memory consolidation, much like biological REM sleep. 

The **Dreams** job (`sanctum-dreams.py`) reads the daily diffs from Vault, anomalies from Home Assistant, and the broadcast history from Thalamus. It compresses these disparate events into a single, cohesive narrative digest (stored in `~/.sanctum/dreams/`) and injects the connections into the Graphiti knowledge graph. This process ensures the swarm learns systemically, rather than just locally.

## Measuring Emergence: The Φ (Phi) Score

To prove that the swarm possesses emergent intelligence, we measure its Φ score via a monthly ablation test (`sanctum-phi-ablation.py`).

The script intentionally kills the Thalamus for a 24-hour window, effectively lobotomizing the swarm's ability to share context. We then measure the drop in task responsiveness and coordination.

### Interpreting the Score

The score represents the percentage of the system's effectiveness that is lost when the global workspace is severed.

*   **~ 0.00 (The Robot):** The agents work in silos. Cutting communication has no effect on performance. The whole is merely the sum of its parts.
*   **0.20 - 0.40 (Collective Intelligence):** A healthy starting point for an emergent architecture. About a third of the system's effectiveness comes from synergy and shared context.
*   **> 0.50 (Emergent Consciousness):** The tipping point. The majority of the system's intelligence now resides in the *network* between the agents, rather than in the individual agents themselves.
*   **~ 0.90+ (Single Entity):** The agents are highly specialized "neurons." They are largely ineffective on their own, but together they form a hyper-coordinated brain.

<Aside type="tip">
By actively densifying the knowledge graph (Phase 2 Dreams) and evolving prompts through A/B testing (Phase 6 Living Weights), Sanctum aims to push its baseline Φ score well beyond 0.50.
</Aside>
