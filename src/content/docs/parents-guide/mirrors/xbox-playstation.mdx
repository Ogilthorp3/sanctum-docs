The Sanctum schedule is enforced at the network: when the evening ends, the haus cuts the connection and Firewalla confirms it — red on failure, never a fake green. For most console play that is already the ball game, because online sessions die with the network. But a downloaded game runs fine offline, and a determined kid discovers this at 22:07 on a school night. A single-player campaign does not care that the Wi-Fi is gone.

That is what a **mirror** is for. You copy the same hours into the console maker's own controls — **Xbox Family Settings** on the Microsoft side, **Gestion de la famille** on the PlayStation side — so the console itself stops when the evening ends. The network enforces from outside; the console enforces from inside; both agree on the hour. Nothing here replaces the Sanctum schedule — this is the second lock on the same door.

## What you are copying

Before opening either app, look at the Sanctum schedule for the child. Note two times: when the evening ends on a **school night**, and when it ends on a **weekend**. Those are the numbers you will type in twice — once per console.

![The schedule screen in French on a phone: layered rows for Semaine, Fin de semaine, Congés and Études, each with its evening end time](../images/shots/schedule.fr.phone.png)

One difference from the Nintendo world is in your favour here: Xbox and PlayStation limits follow the **child's account**, not the console. If Alex and Sam share one machine, each gets their own limit — as long as each signs in as themselves. A child playing on a sibling's profile inherits the sibling's clock, so separate accounts are the first thing to check, not the last.

## Xbox — Family Settings

You manage everything from the **Xbox Family Settings** app on your own phone, signed in with your Microsoft account. The child's account must be in your family group; the app walks you through adding it the first time.

<Steps>

1. Install the **Xbox Family Settings** app and sign in with the parent Microsoft account. Add the child's account to the family group if it is not there yet — the console will confirm on its side.

2. Open the child's profile and choose **Temps d'écran** (Screen time). Set a schedule for each day: the *Semaine* end time on school nights, the *Fin de semaine* time on Saturday and Sunday. Xbox lets you set both a daily total and a window of allowed hours — the window is the part that mirrors the Sanctum bedtime.

3. When the time is up, the console suspends play and asks a parent to approve more. Approvals arrive on your phone. Treat that button the way you treat Sanctum's *More Time* flow — which is capped at 120 minutes and logged. Microsoft's approval has no cap, so the discipline is yours: when more time is deserved, grant it in Sanctum first, then approve the same amount on the phone.

4. If the child also plays on a Windows PC with the same account, the same limits can cover it. One schedule, both screens — set it once in the app.

</Steps>

## PlayStation — Gestion de la famille

PlayStation's controls live under **Gestion de la famille** (Family Management), reachable from the PlayStation App on your phone or from the console under **Paramètres → Famille et contrôle parental**. Your account must be the family manager; the child's account joins as a family member.

<Steps>

1. From the PlayStation App or the console settings, open **Gestion de la famille** and select the child's account.

2. Open **Temps de jeu** (Playtime Settings). Set the daily duration and the playable hours for each day — again the *Semaine* end time on school nights, the *Fin de semaine* time on weekends.

3. Find the setting for what happens when time runs out, and set it to **log the child out of the game** when the limit is reached — not "notify only". This toggle is the whole mirror. On "notify only", the console shows a small message and play continues past bedtime; on log-out, the session ends on the spot.

4. Extra time is granted from the family manager's account, per request. Same rule as the Xbox side: mirror Sanctum's grant, do not replace it. Sanctum's cap is 120 minutes and every grant is logged; keep the console grants matching what Sanctum already approved.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Save first, always">
A forced log-out at the limit can cost unsaved progress in games without autosave. The consoles warn a few minutes ahead — teach the kids that the warning means *save now*, not *negotiate now*. It is a better lesson learned at 21:55 than at 22:00.
</Aside>

## What the mirror does not change

The Sanctum side keeps working exactly as before. Manual blocks still expire at next wake by default; nothing blocks forever. A pause still asks who it is for and for how long, up to *jusqu'au réveil*. Budgets still meter at the network, per child across all their devices, by category — and stay in shadow (observe-only) mode until you arm them. Every hold still shows its owner and its expiry, and every block is still Firewalla-confirmed. The console mirrors cover the one case the network cannot: a downloaded game running offline past the hour. If the two ever disagree, the stricter one wins in practice, because each enforces on its own.

## Sanctum surveille ce miroir

A hand-made copy drifts. You will push bedtime later for the holidays, add a layer for the relâche, and both consoles will quietly keep the old hours. You do not have to remember this — Sanctum does. The app knows what the haus schedule says, and when that schedule changes, it flags this mirror as **stale** in the app — a plain marker telling you the Xbox and PlayStation copies no longer match. Update the times in the two apps, and the flag clears. The mirror stays a mirror instead of slowly becoming a souvenir.

*Yes, that is three places to change one bedtime. The flag exists so you only have to remember zero of them.*
