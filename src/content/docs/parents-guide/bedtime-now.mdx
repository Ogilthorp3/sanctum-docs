It is 22:05. The curfew argument has been lost — or technically won, just not observed. **Dodo maintenant** (bedtime now) is the button for this exact moment: it ends the evening on the spot instead of waiting for the schedule to get around to it.

## One child: Dodo maintenant

Every child's card on the Home screen (Accueil) carries its own **Dodo maintenant** button, right under the +15 / +30 / Devoirs row.

![The Home screen in French on a phone: a card for each child — Alex active with 36 minutes left before curfew, Sam already winding down — each with its own Dodo maintenant button, and the haus-wide Pause internet button underneath](./images/shots/home.fr.phone.png)

Tap it and that child's night starts now. It is a pause like any other — every pause needs a *who* and a *how long* — but this button answers both questions for you: the who is that card's child, and the how long is **jusqu'au réveil**, until their next wake time, which is the longest any block can be. Every screen that child uses — phone, PC, console, the shared TV — goes down together. The other child's evening is untouched, and parents' devices are never on the list at all.

## The whole haus at once

When it is everyone's bedtime, skip the per-card buttons and use the big **Pause internet** button at the bottom of the same screen.

<Steps>

1. Tap **Pause internet**.

2. Under **Pour qui?**, choose **Tous les enfants**.

3. Under **Pour combien de temps?**, choose **Jusqu'au réveil**. A duration is always required — *Souper 1 h* and *Personnalisé* are the other options, and there is deliberately no "forever".

4. Tap **Mettre en pause**.

</Steps>

![The Pause internet sheet in French on a phone: first who — all the kids, Alex, or Sam — then how long — the supper hour, until wake, or a custom duration — with Mettre en pause to confirm](./images/shots/pause-sheet.fr.phone.png)

Each child is released at their own wake time, so an early riser is not held hostage by a sibling who sleeps in.

## What the kids' screens actually do

Worth knowing before the first protest reaches your bedroom door. The block is enforced at the network, not by reaching into the device, so:

- **The screen does not go black.** Nothing shuts down, nothing is deleted, no game save is lost. The device is fine; the internet has simply stopped answering it.
- **Online things stop.** Streams stall, online matches drop back to their menus, pages stop loading. Anything that needs the network is over for the night.
- **The app can tell you why.** Open the device's card while a block is active and the hold is spelled out: what is blocked, who blocked it, and exactly when it ends. Every hold shows its owner and its expiry — no mystery outages, and no "the Wi-Fi is broken" debates.
- **Confirmed, not assumed.** A card only says *bloqué* once the network box has confirmed the block on that device. If a device does not comply, its card turns red and says so in plain words — it never shows you a fake green.

<Aside type="note">
If a card lingers unconfirmed for a few seconds after you tap, the app is just waiting for the network box before it claims victory. A card that stays red names the device that did not comply — the Troubleshooting page in this guide covers what to do next.
</Aside>

## And in the morning

Nothing to remember, nothing to undo. A manual block expires on its own at the child's next wake time by default — **nothing blocks forever**. The morning unblocks the screens the same way it unblocks the child.

If something genuinely cannot wait until then — a homework file left in the cloud, a call that has to happen — an override can punch a temporary hole through the pause. That lives on the More Time page and is capped at 120 minutes, so tonight's exception cannot quietly become tomorrow's rule.

The button handles the screens. The glass of water, the second glass of water, and the sudden bedtime interest in philosophy remain out of scope.
